# Principles

These principles outrank features, deadlines and technical convenience. When two of them collide, the earlier one wins.

## 1. Knowledge belongs to its holders

Communities and individuals who hold knowledge decide **who may see it, how it is attributed, and whether AI may use it**. These controls are in the core data model ([knowledge sovereignty](../architecture/knowledge-sovereignty.md)), not bolted on. We align with the [CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance](https://www.gida-global.org/care) — Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics — and complement them with FAIR practices for the data that *is* open.

## 2. Safety before cleverness

Plants heal and plants kill. A confident wrong answer is worse than no answer. The system shows confidence honestly, flags toxic species and dangerous look-alikes prominently, and never presents traditional use as medical instruction. See the [safety model](../architecture/safety-model.md).

## 3. Human first, AI assisted

AI proposes; people with the relevant authority decide. The AI **never invents tradition**, never silently "fills in" a gap, and never publishes community knowledge without human review. Every AI-produced output is labelled as such.

## 4. Every claim has a source

No orphan facts. A claim is traceable to a contributor, a community, or a published source — and the user can see which.

## 5. Free, forever, for people

No ads. No paywalls on knowledge. No selling or brokering of user or community data. Sustainability comes from grants, donations and institutional partnerships.

## 6. Languages are first-class

Every language is presented in its own orthography, reviewed by its own speakers. Low-resource languages get priority and investment, not an "also available in…" afterthought. Machine translation is a draft for a human custodian, never the final word.

## 7. Offline by default, light by design

Built for 2–3 GB Android phones, patchy coverage and expensive data. Core features work without a connection. Heavy features degrade gracefully.

## 8. Protect the living things too

Locations of harvest-sensitive, threatened or protected species are obscured. The project promotes sustainable harvesting and conservation, never over-collection.

## 9. Openness with care

Code, schemas, docs and decisions are open. Community knowledge is **open only where its holders choose**; "open by default" does not apply to other people's heritage.

## 10. Build slowly where it matters

Speed is fine for UI. For consent, safety and attribution we move deliberately, with the right people in the room.
